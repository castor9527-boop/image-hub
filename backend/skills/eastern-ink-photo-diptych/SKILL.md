# Eastern Ink Photo Diptych

Create one finished vertical poster from one user-supplied photograph. Treat the source as evidence: preserve it above and interpret it below.

## Workflow

1. Inspect the source image before editing. Identify orientation, horizon or dominant spatial division, principal silhouette, negative space, repeated masses, and the source palette.
2. Use the image-generation/editing tool with the source image attached. Do not reconstruct the upper photograph from text alone.
3. Generate the complete poster in one pass when practical. Request a vertical canvas, preferably 3:5 unless the user specifies another ratio.
4. Inspect the result. Regenerate if the upper photograph changes materially, the lower illustration becomes detailed or decorative, new colors appear, text is misspelled, or the composition loses its quiet negative space.
5. Deliver the final raster image and briefly state the chosen title. Do not claim exact photographic preservation if the generation tool could only approximate it.

## Composition

- Use a vertical two-part layout on matte warm ivory art paper.
- Place the faithful source photograph across the upper portion, normally 38–48% of the usable height. Preserve its native crop where possible; use a restrained crop only to fit the panel.
- Preserve the original subject arrangement, architectural outline, landform, sky-to-ground or water proportion, spatial rhythm, and subdued natural color.
- Place the distilled ink illustration below, normally 38–48% of the usable height.
- Reserve a quiet bottom margin for a small centered English title and optional shorter subtitle.
- Keep margins generous, alignment deliberate, and the transition between panels clean or softly dissolved. Do not add frames, badges, ornaments, seals, mockup shadows, or decorative borders.

## Upper photographic panel

- Retain real photographic texture and believable optics.
- Do not move, add, delete, beautify, or redesign buildings, mountains, trees, water, roads, people, or other meaningful subjects.
- Do not invent weather, dramatic light, fog, reflections, or cinematic grading.
- Allow only conservative global corrections needed for legibility: slight exposure, contrast, white-balance, or noise adjustment.

## Lower ink abstraction

- Distill the source scene into its few most important visual signs: limited architecture contours, landform direction, skyline ratio, water edge, dominant vegetation masses, or one tiny focal subject when indispensable.
- Recompose geometrically rather than copying every detail.
- Use large areas of blank paper, broad flat or softly bleeding shapes, few layers, and a calm architectural-study rhythm.
- Suggest trees and vegetation as grouped masses; never render individual leaves, bark, tiles, paving, fabric, faces, or textures.
- Use pale ink bloom, rice-paper translucency, soft edges, and low-contrast overlaps. Avoid hard outlines, complex chiaroscuro, photorealism, and busy watercolor detail.
- Preserve only subdued colors sampled or inferred directly from the photograph, such as rice white, muted grey-green, blue-grey, warm ochre, or soft brown. Do not introduce an unrelated accent color.

## Typography

- Add one short, scene-responsive English title in a refined thin serif, small uppercase or restrained title case, with generous tracking.
- Optionally add one even smaller subtitle only when it improves the editorial reading.
- Keep type dark warm grey or a source-derived near-neutral. Ensure spelling is exact.
- If reliable text rendering is unavailable, prefer leaving a clean title area over producing garbled text; add the title in a deterministic layout tool when available.

## Generation prompt scaffold

Adapt this scaffold to the source rather than pasting it mechanically:

> Create a vertical 3:5 editorial art-paper poster from the attached photograph. Use a quiet two-part composition on matte warm ivory paper. In the upper panel, retain the source photograph faithfully, preserving the original scene arrangement, subject silhouettes, architecture or landform, spatial proportions, and subdued natural palette; make no invented changes and keep authentic photographic texture. In the lower panel, reinterpret the same scene as Eastern minimalist ink-and-wash architectural abstraction. Keep only the essential spatial signs and dominant masses, geometrically simplify them, and use expansive negative space, few flat translucent shapes, pale ink bloom, rice-paper permeability, soft edges, and low detail. Use only muted colors derived from the source. Do not draw individual leaves, tiles, roads, textures, people, decorations, sharp outlines, dramatic light, or complex shadows. Add a tiny elegant thin-serif English title in the bottom margin: “[TITLE]”. The result should feel like a restrained high-end Eastern architecture or landscape study booklet: clean, rational, calm, and spacious.

## Acceptance checks

- Upper panel remains recognizably and spatially faithful to the supplied photograph.
- Lower panel reads as the same scene after abstraction, not as an unrelated painting.
- Detail density decreases decisively from top to bottom.
- Palette comes from the source and remains low saturation.
- Negative space, proportion, and rhythm carry the design.
- No extra decorative elements or illegible typography appear.
